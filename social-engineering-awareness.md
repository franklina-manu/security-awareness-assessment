# Social Engineering Awareness

## What is social engineering?
Social engineering is the use of deception or manipulation to persuade someone to disclose information, provide access, transfer money or take an unsafe action.

Attackers may use email, phone calls, text messages, messaging apps, social media or in-person approaches.

## Common techniques
### Phishing
Fraudulent messages designed to trick people into clicking a link, opening an attachment or sharing information.

### Spear phishing
A targeted phishing attempt tailored to a particular person, role or organisation.

### Pretexting
Someone invents a believable story or identity to obtain information or access.

### Impersonation
A person pretends to be a colleague, manager, supplier, bank or IT support worker.

### Tailgating
An unauthorised person follows an authorised person into a restricted area.

## Warning signs
- A request is unexpected or outside normal procedure.
- The person pressures you to act immediately.
- They ask you to keep the request secret.
- They request passwords, MFA codes, customer data or payment.
- They ask you to bypass approvals or physical access controls.
- Their identity cannot be verified independently.

## How to respond
1. Pause. Do not let urgency override normal security checks.
2. Verify the person's identity through a known, separate channel.
3. Follow approved payment, access and information-sharing procedures.
4. Do not share passwords, MFA codes or confidential information.
5. Report suspicious approaches to the security team.

## Scenario for discussion
A caller claims to be from IT support and says they need your MFA code to fix an urgent account issue.

**Correct response:** Do not share the code. End the call and contact IT through the published internal support channel. Report the call.

## Key message
Be helpful, but verify first. Legitimate staff should understand and respect security checks.
