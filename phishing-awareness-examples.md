# Phishing Awareness: Fictional Examples

## Purpose
These examples help employees practise spotting suspicious messages. They are fictional training examples and must not be used to impersonate real organisations or collect credentials.

## Example 1: Unexpected account warning
**Subject:** Action required: mailbox access review

**Message:**
> Hello,
>
> Our fictional IT team has detected an issue with your mailbox. Please review the message carefully and contact the IT Service Desk using the phone number or support portal already published in the company directory.
>
> Thank you,  
> SecureCorp IT Awareness Team

**Training discussion — warning signs to look for**
- Unexpected account warnings can create urgency or fear.
- A message may use a sender display name that does not match its actual address.
- Links and attachments should be treated cautiously, especially when unexpected.
- Employees should navigate to known company services themselves rather than use an unverified link.

**Safe response:** Do not open unexpected attachments or enter credentials from a message link. Verify the request through a known IT contact or approved service portal.

## Example 2: Unexpected invoice attachment
**Subject:** Invoice overdue — immediate review

**Message:**
> Hello Finance Team,
>
> Please review the attached invoice. The sender and transaction have not yet been verified.

**Training discussion — warning signs to look for**
- An unexpected attachment.
- Pressure to act quickly.
- A request that does not match normal purchasing or payment procedures.
- Missing or unusual business context.

**Safe response:** Confirm the invoice with the known supplier contact and follow the organisation's payment approval process. Do not enable macros or bypass security warnings.

## Example 3: Executive impersonation
**Subject:** Confidential request

**Message:**
> I am in a meeting and cannot speak. Please purchase gift cards and send the codes immediately.

**Training discussion — warning signs to look for**
- Unusual request from someone claiming to be senior.
- Secrecy and urgency.
- Request to bypass normal approval controls.
- A payment method that is inconsistent with company policy.

**Safe response:** Verify using a separate, trusted communication channel and follow normal approval procedures.

## Common phishing indicators
- Sender address is unfamiliar, misspelled or inconsistent with the claimed organisation.
- Unexpected links, QR codes or attachments.
- Urgent threats, unusual rewards or pressure to keep the request secret.
- Requests for passwords, MFA codes, payment or sensitive information.
- Poorly matched context, unexpected language or unusual payment instructions.

## What to do if you receive a suspicious message
1. Do not click links, scan QR codes, open attachments or reply.
2. Use the organisation's approved reporting method, such as the email client’s Report Phishing feature or the IT/security help desk.
3. Do not forward the message widely.
4. If you interacted with it, report that fact promptly and follow the incident-response instructions.
5. If credentials may have been exposed, contact IT/security immediately. Change credentials only through the organisation's trusted sign-in route and follow its instructions.

## Discussion questions
- What makes each example suspicious?
- Which details would you verify?
- What approved reporting channel should an employee use?
